#### 7. DETERMINING WINNERS

a. Each Submission will be scored and ranked using OCF (Oriented Contour Fidelity), on a scale from 0 to 100 where higher is better. The score for each task is the arithmetic mean of its per-image OCF scores, and the final score is the equal-weighted average of the three task scores, as described in Overview > Evaluation. During the Competition Period, the current ranking will be visible on the Competition Website's Public Leaderboard. The potential winner(s) are determined by each Team's best valid Public Leaderboard score achieved before the Final Submission Deadline, subject to compliance with these Rules. All 30,200 test samples (2,720 in Task 1, 5,496 in Task 2, and 21,984 in Task 3) are used to calculate the Public Leaderboard score. No separate held-out private test set is used.

b. In the event of a tie, the Submission that was entered first to the Competition will be the winner. In the event a potential winner is disqualified for any reason, the Submission that received the next highest score rank will be chosen as the potential winner.

#### 18. DEFINITIONS

**"Competition Data"** are the data or datasets available from the Competition Website for the purpose of use in the Competition, including any prototype or executable code provided on the Competition Website. All 30,200 leaderboard test samples are used for public scoring. Ground-truth test targets are not made available to Participants. No separate held-out private test set is used.

An **“Entry”** is when a Participant has joined, signed up, or accepted the rules of a competition. Entry is required to make a Submission to a competition.

**“Final Submission”** means the valid Submission selected by a Team for judging before the Final Submission Deadline. If the Team does not make a selection by that deadline, its valid Submission with the highest Public Leaderboard score will be automatically selected. The Public Leaderboard score of the Final Submission is used to determine the Team’s final prize ranking.

**“Participant”** or **“Participant User”** is an individual who participates in a competition by entering the competition and making a Submission.

**“Public Leaderboard”** is a ranked display of Participants’ Submission scores against all leaderboard test samples. This leaderboard is visible throughout the Competition and determines final prize rankings as specified in Section C(7).

**“Sponsor”** is responsible for hosting the competition, which includes but is not limited to providing the data for the competition, determining winners, and enforcing competition rules.

**“Submission”** is anything provided by the Participant to the Sponsor to be evaluated for competition purposes and determine leaderboard position. A Submission uploaded to Kaggle must be a CSV file, or a ZIP archive containing that CSV file, with exactly 30,200 data rows and the columns `sample_id` and `prediction`. Each prediction must be the standard base64 encoding of one 64 × 64, 8-bit, single-channel grayscale PNG (mode="L") for the corresponding test sample. The participant package generates a PNG archive named `submission.zip`, with `task1/`, `task2/`, and `task3/` at its root; Participants must convert that archive using the supplied lossless Kaggle submission adapter and upload the resulting `kaggle_submission.zip`. This conversion changes only the submission container, not the image pixels or evaluation methodology, as specified in Overview > Evaluation and Data.

**“Team”** is one or more Participants participating together in a Kaggle competition, by officially merging together as a Team within the competition platform.
